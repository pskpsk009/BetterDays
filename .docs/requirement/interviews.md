# User Interviews - Better Days Wellbeing Companion

Real target-user interviews used to confirm or revise the pains (P1-P3) in
`backlog.md`.

**Six interviews (I1-I6) were completed on 2/11/2026 as an online survey
shared through a social app. All six participants gave consent.** Answers below are
copied word-for-word from the interview notes, including the participants' own
spelling and grammar. Fields that were not recorded are marked
"Not recorded". Nothing has been invented or reworded.

## Privacy rules for this file (PDPA)

- Ask for verbal or written consent before the interview and record it below.
- Do not write names, phone numbers, student IDs, or social media handles.
  Use codes I1-I6 only.
- Do not record health diagnoses or anything that identifies a person.
- Delete raw notes or recordings once this file is filled in, unless the
  participant agreed to keep them.

## Target user

Students and young adults (about 18-30) who want to improve daily wellbeing
habits such as sleep, stress, social connection, and activity.

## Interview script

Read before starting:

> "We are a student team building a wellbeing habit app. This interview takes
> about 15 minutes. There are no right or wrong answers. We will not record
> your name, and you can skip any question or stop at any time. Is it okay to
> continue and take notes?"

### Questions

**Background**

1. What do you do (study year, work)? Roughly what is your age range?
2. What does a normal weekday look like for you?

**Habits and awareness (P1)**

3. How would you describe your sleep, stress, and energy over the past two
   weeks?
4. Do you notice patterns, for example what makes a day good or bad? How do
   you notice them?

**Advice and next steps (P2)**

5. When you want to feel better or build a habit, where do you look for
   advice?
6. Was that advice easy to act on? Why or why not?

**Tracking and consistency (P3)**

7. Have you ever used an app, journal, or notes to track mood, sleep, or
   habits? What happened?
8. What made you stop, or what keeps you going?

**Wrap-up**

9. What is the hardest part about keeping healthy habits for you?
10. If an app could help with one thing, what should it be?
11. Anything about privacy that would stop you from using a wellbeing app?

## Summary

Six interviews, 2/11/2026. A pain is **confirmed** when at least half of the
participants (3 of 6) mention it.

### Pain frequency

| Pain | Description | Mentioned by | Count | Confirmed? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Lack of self-awareness about patterns across sleep, stress, connection, activity, and daily functioning | I2, I3 (I1 partial) | 2/6 | No. I4, I5, and I6 clearly linked good days to enough sleep |
| P2 | Unclear next steps because general wellbeing advice is too broad or difficult to apply | I1, I3, I4 (I2 partial) | 3/6 | **Yes** |
| P3 | Inconsistent tracking because check-ins, reflections, goals, and advice are kept in separate places | None | 0/6 | No. No participant had ever used a tracking app or journal |
| P4 (new) | Poor or irregular sleep that affects stress and energy | I1, I2, I3, I4, I5, I6 | 6/6 | **Yes, strongest pain** |
| P5 (new) | Hard to stay consistent with healthy habits over time | I1, I3, I4, I6 | 4/6 | **Yes** |
| P6 (new) | Worry that personal or sensitive data could leak | I1, I4, I5, I6 | 4/6 | **Yes** |
| P7 (new) | Expect wellbeing apps to cost money | I5 | 1/6 | No |

### Key insights

1. **Sleep is the main problem.** All six participants mentioned sleep, and
   four named it when asked what an app should help with (I3, I4, I5, I6).
   Several linked poor sleep directly to stress and low energy.
2. **The problem is consistency, not scattered tracking.** Nobody had ever used
   a tracking app or journal, so P3 as written is not supported. What they
   described was failing to keep a habit going (I1: "it only last for few
   weeks").
3. **People get advice from screens, not people.** Sources were social media,
   Google, YouTube, Reddit, TikTok, and AI chatbots (ChatGPT, Grok, Gemini).
   Generic advice was hard to act on. Clear step-by-step advice worked (I6).
4. **Privacy is a real barrier.** Four of six named a specific data leak fear:
   private confessions (I1), location and ID numbers (I4), and body data
   (I5).
